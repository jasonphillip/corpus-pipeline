# Hardware Note: Helical Antenna LS-ANT-0090

Synthetic document, Larkspur Systems wiki (fictional).

The LS-ANT-0090 is the standard receiving antenna. It is a helical antenna with right-hand circular polarization. The helix matches the polarization of most sonde transmitters, which reduces fading as the balloon swings.

## Specifications

- Frequency range: 400 MHz to 406 MHz.
- Gain: 11 dBi at 404 MHz.
- Half-power beamwidth: 45 degrees.
- Axial ratio: under 2 dB on the boresight.
- Weight: 1.8 kg.
- Wind rating: 130 km/h survival, with the antenna aimed into the wind.
- Length: 1.2 m.
- Connector: N-type female at the feed.

## Mounting

The antenna clamps to a 50 mm diameter mast pipe. The standard mast is 4 m tall and made of galvanized steel. The antenna boom has a mark for the center of gravity. Clamp the boom at the mark.

Tilt the antenna to the target elevation. For most sites the target is 15 degrees above the horizon, because balloons spend the first minutes of flight at low elevation and then climb. See the antenna realign runbook for tolerances and torque values.

## Why a narrow beam

A beamwidth of 45 degrees keeps out noise from the sides. The balloon stays inside the beam for most of a flight because the launch site is far away and the balloon drifts roughly along the line of sight. A balloon that drifts across the beam moves out of it after about 20 minutes. The standby site then takes over the track.

## Care

- Inspect the feed connector every 6 months for water.
- Clean bird droppings from the helix. They detune the antenna.
- Do not paint the helix.

## Ice

Heavy ice on the helix cuts gain by up to 6 dB. Ice melts off in a few hours at most sites. A site that loses signal during ice storms is usually not broken.

## Spares

The stock room keeps 4 antennas. Each ships with its test sheet that lists the measured return loss. A good unit shows return loss better than 15 dB across the band.
